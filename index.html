Can you really make money online?

Yes, people earn income online in several legitimate ways — including services, digital products, content, ecommerce and affiliate marketing. None of these are guaranteed income, and each requires a real value exchange, time, skill, audience, systems or some combination of them.

Is affiliate marketing legitimate?

Yes. Affiliate marketing is a business model in which a company may pay a commission when an eligible customer purchases through an affiliate’s referral. The important part is choosing reputable programs, disclosing affiliate relationships and never treating commissions as guaranteed income.

Do you need thousands or millions of followers?

No fixed follower count guarantees success. A smaller audience that cares about the problem you address can be more useful than a large audience with little interest. GoodGround focuses on relevance, trust and useful content rather than chasing vanity numbers.

So where should you start?

Start with the problem, not the product. Decide who you want to help, understand the problem they are trying to solve, learn the available options and choose one small next step you can actually test.

WHY GOODGROUND EXISTS

GoodGround was born from the desire to learn, build something of my own and make the online path clearer for people starting with the same questions I had.

My name is Jaqueline Martins. For 24 years, I worked in public service as a nursing technician, much of that time inside an operating room. It was a demanding career, and after many years I reached a point where I knew I wanted something different for my life.

I began studying online business, affiliate marketing and new ways of working. At first, I had many of the same questions people still have today: Where do I start? What is legitimate? What actually works? What is worth learning? What should I avoid?

Along the way, I also spent money on courses that promised to teach online business, but in some cases the main thing they taught was how to sell that same course to someone else. That made me ask harder questions: Was I really learning how to build something? Was I learning a real skill? Was I solving a real problem for someone? Or was I just being taught to repeat the same sales cycle?

The more I studied, the more I realized how confusing this world can be when you are starting from zero. That is how GoodGround was born — from the desire to learn, to build something of my own and to create a different path.

Along the way, I gained knowledge, experience and perspective. Now I want to share what I continue learning with people who have the same doubts I once had. GoodGround is not about pretending to have all the answers. It is about researching, learning, explaining things clearly and helping people make better-informed decisions.

Learn first. Decide later.
